# Field-of-View Strategies for eXtended Reality Navigation with Dynamic Peripheral Real-World Views
Passthrough-enabled Mixed Reality (XR) navigation systems can maintain peripheral real-world visibility while users interact with central virtual content. When Peripheral Real-World View (PRV) is adopted as a system feature, designers must determine how FoV modulation and locomotion should be configured within that con- text. We conducted a 2 × 2 within-subject study with 22 partic- ipants to characterize Fixed and Dynamic FoV strategies across Controller and Treadmill locomotion setups in a PRV-enabled nav- igation system. Motion-contingent PRV was maintained as a com- mon design premise across all conditions rather than manipulated as an experimental factor. Dynamic FoV produced higher rat- ings of peripheral-change noticeability than Fixed FoV. Controller also yielded a higher object-recognition hit rate than Treadmill; however, no corresponding differences were found in sensitivity, false-alarm rate, or response criterion, providing no clear evidence of superior overall recognition performance. No statistically de- tectable differences in cybersickness were observed across condi- tions. Overall, the findings indicate that, within the tested PRV- enabled system, the clearest consequence of Dynamic FoV was in- creased intervention noticeability rather than detectable improve- ments in task performance or cybersickness. These results provide conditional evidence for configuring FoV strategies and locomotion setups after PRV has been adopted as a system requirement.
